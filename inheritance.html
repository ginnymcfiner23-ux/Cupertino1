Execution plan / 04

How a trust inheritance should be handled

This is a general sequence, not a legal determination that the assets are held in a trust or avoid probate. The trust document, title, ownership form, family relationship, death date, and county-specific assessor instructions control. Engage a California trust/estate attorney and CPA promptly.

Deadline caution: For California property, inherited-property change in ownership may be effective on the date of death—not the later trust distribution. Start assessor and Prop 19 deadline tracking immediately, even if the trustee has not yet distributed title.
At transfer

Step-by-step checklist

Keep a dated record of filings and confirmations.

Secure authority and protect the property

Obtain the current trust and amendments, will, deeds, insurance policies, mortgage/loan information, leases, tax bills and relevant account statements. Confirm who is the acting successor trustee and what authority they have; do not rely on an unsigned draft or informal family understanding. Maintain utilities and sufficient appropriate insurance; notify insurers about a death, vacancy, rental occupancy or ownership change and get written coverage confirmation.

Verify title, trust and authority with counsel; policies/insurance needs are contract-specific. California Courts probate overview · California DOI insurance guide

Identify the property transfer route

Have an estate attorney verify how each property is titled, whether it is trust-owned, joint tenancy, community property, probate property, or subject to a mortgage, and whether there are other interests or liens. A revocable living trust may direct administration outside probate for trust assets, but trust wording and title must be checked. If probate is needed, follow the Superior Court process.

California Courts: probate · SCC Assessor: trust ownership / death FAQ

Notify each county Assessor and report the death

For San Francisco, the Death of Real Property Owner / Change in Ownership Statement is generally due within 150 days after death, or within 90 days of written request; probate cases have a separate filing point. Santa Clara County also instructs transferees to file a Death Statement within 150 days. Ask the relevant Assessor which forms are required for each title/trust and keep filing receipts.

County deadlines are independent of the trust's distribution schedule. SF Assessor official process · SCC Assessor annual report (death statement guidance)

Appraise each asset and determine federal estate-tax filings

Obtain defensible date-of-death valuations for real estate, financial assets, business interests, life insurance where includible, and other property. Determine gross estate, deductible claims/expenses, adjusted taxable lifetime gifts, spouse portability, and whether Form 706 is required or beneficial. For a 2026 death, IRS gives a $15,000,000 filing threshold; that number will not be assumed for a death in 2040–2050. Form 706 is generally due nine months after death; request an extension if needed. A portability election requires a timely complete Form 706.

IRS estate-tax FAQ · IRS Form 706 instructions · IRS Pub. 559

Protect and document the inherited tax basis

Record date-of-death fair market value and supporting appraisal, asset allocation, trust/estate basis schedules, capital improvements, depreciation history, and sale/transfer costs. Inherited-property basis is generally date-of-death fair market value, subject to exceptions. Do not use market listing estimates as a substitute for an appraisal or estate records.

IRS Pub. 551 · IRS Pub. 559

Make the Prop 19 decision before deadlines

If potentially eligible for a parent-child (or qualifying grandparent-grandchild) family-home exclusion, confirm transferor principal-residence status, recipient relationship, trust transfer, transfer-date assessed value and market value cap, and which recipient will occupy the family home. BOE guidance requires the transferee to make it a principal residence within one year and generally file the homeowners’ exemption within one year. File BOE-19-P within three years or before a third-party transfer. County assessors decide the claim. The 2025–2027 cap is $1,044,586 plus the transferor's factored base-year value; later amounts are not yet known.

CA BOE Prop 19 fact sheet · 2025 cap adjustment · SF Assessor Prop 19 guidance

Transfer title and confirm assessor / tax-bill processing

Work with the trustee, attorney, title company and recorder to execute and record the required affidavits/deeds or probate orders. Submit any applicable preliminary/change-in-ownership reports, then verify recorded ownership and the Assessor’s enrolled assessed value. Check for supplemental assessments, property tax bills, homeowner exemption and deadlines. A recording alone does not establish that a Prop 19 exclusion was granted.

SF Recorder filing information · SCC parcel records

Choose live / rent / sell using verified after-tax numbers

Build a 10-year and 2040–2050 scenario only when transfer facts and reliable inputs exist. Compare after-tax net sale proceeds against expected net rent and carrying costs; include the Prop 19 reassessment result, capital gains, repairs/capex, insurance, utilities, tenant law, selling costs, investment taxes, fees, inflation and risk. Document whether SF remains rented, whether the vacant unit can be occupied, and whether any units are rent-controlled or subject to protected status.

IRS home-sale rules · IRS rental tax rules · SF Rent Board rules

File income-tax returns and administer final distributions

File the decedent’s final individual returns and any estate/trust income-tax returns that are required; report post-death rents and other income. Use IRS Publication 559 and Form 1041 instructions with a CPA. Reconcile liabilities, distributions, and beneficiary statements before final trust distribution; retain the records and appraisals.

IRS Pub. 559 · IRS Form 1041 instructions
Before the future event

Preparation to reduce cost and uncertainty

Preserve evidence securely

Maintain current deeds, trust schedules, tax returns, prior gift Form 709 records, any estate Form 706/DSUE election, property tax bills, insurance policies, permits, lease documents, and improvement invoices in secure estate records—not this public website.

IRS estate records context

Review trust and Prop 19 choices

A trust plan should be reviewed for current law and beneficiaries' actual residence choices. Trust transfers do not automatically avoid property-tax reassessment; the effect depends on who benefits and when ownership changes.

CA BOE Prop 19 guidance

Refresh numbers annually

Update asset inventory, assessed value, coverage limits, rent roll, and operating costs annually. Recalculate gift/estate law, indexed thresholds and deadlines at the date of any gift or death. No economic projection is reliable to 2040–2050 without explicit scenario assumptions.
