Federal income tax · basis

Inherited property basis and sale taxes

Starting basis

For inherited property, basis is generally fair market value at date of death (or alternate valuation if elected), subject to statutory exceptions. Secure a qualified date-of-death appraisal, allocation between land/building and unit uses, and estate basis reporting. Do not use today's estimate as a future basis.

IRS Pub. 551, inherited property

Sale and residence exclusion

A qualifying owner-occupied home sale may exclude up to $250,000 of gain, or $500,000 for eligible joint filers, if section 121 requirements are met (generally ownership and use as a main home for 2 of the 5 years before sale, plus other limits). Rental use, depreciation, multi-unit allocation, and timing can change the result. Do not assume the full gain is excluded.

Local tax facts and assessed-value checks

Santa Clara County

County Assessor identifies property and establishes assessment, but does not compute/collect the property-tax bill or set rates. The parcel search provides assessed value and APN when searched privately by an authorized researcher; this public dashboard omits those identifying details.

San Francisco

SF Assessor-Recorder guidance states death may trigger reassessment unless an exclusion applies; a Death of Real Property Owner statement is generally due within 150 days of death or 90 days after a written request, with separate probate timing. Details differ by title, probate, and trust terms.

Rental / occupancy

SF rental tax and tenant-law considerations

Rental tax reporting

Residential rental income and deductible expenses generally are reported on IRS Schedule E; depreciation rules apply to income-producing property. Mixed personal/rental use can require expense allocation. Maintain lease, rent receipt, utility, repair, tax and depreciation records. Rental taxes and basis are fact-specific.

IRS Pub. 527

Owner occupancy is not an automatic right to displace tenants

San Francisco’s Rent Board publishes restrictions and process for owner/relative move-in evictions, including one-unit-per-building limitations and protected-tenant provisions. Whether a specific unit/property is covered or a vacant unit can be occupied depends on its legal status, rental history, and facts. Do not assume a lease can be ended because ownership changed.
