JurisdictionInheritance tax (paid by heir)Estate tax (paid by estate)Source

Other statesFive states tax heirs (KY, MD, NE, NJ, PA); CA is not one of them12 states + DC have estate taxes; CA is not one of themCA FTB · Note: an heir living in, or property located in, an inheritance-tax state may still owe that state's tax

What this means for this inheritance

  • California charges nothing to inherit money or real estate; the inheritance itself is not taxable income to the heir in California or federally. [FTB] Income produced later by inherited assets (rent, interest, dividends) is taxable when received.
  • Federal estate tax applies only if the total estate exceeds the exemption; it is paid by the estate before anything is distributed to heirs — heirs do not receive a bill. [IRS]
  • If the inherited cash plus other estate assets could exceed ≈ $15,000,000 per person, the estate may owe federal estate tax at up to 40% on the excess. For a married couple with portability, up to ≈ $30,000,000 can be sheltered (requires a timely Form 706 by the first spouse's estate).

Rate above the exemption: graduated 18%–40%, effectively 40% on taxable estates over the threshold (IRC § 2001(c)). Form 706 due 9 months after death; portability election requires a timely return. Annual gift tax exclusion: $19,000 per recipient for 2026 (lifetime taxable gifts reduce the same exclusion).

3. Step-up in basis (capital gains when inherited property is later sold)

RuleEffectSource
Basis of inherited property Generally resets to fair market value at date of death (or alternate valuation date, 6 months later, if the estate elects) IRS Pub 551 · Basis of Assets · IRS Pub 559 · Survivors, Executors, and Administrators (IRC § 1014)
Capital gain on later sale Only appreciation after death is taxable; sell soon after inheritance at ≈ date-of-death value → little or no capital gains tax; inherited capital assets are treated as long-term regardless of holding period IRS Pub 551 · IRS Pub 559
Consistent basis reporting If the estate files Form 706 / Schedule A (Form 8971), the heir must use the same value as reported on the estate tax return IRS Pub 559

Why this matters for the Cupertino house

Buying decades ago at a low price, then inheriting at ≈ $3.1M market value: the heir's basis steps up to ≈ $3.1M. Selling shortly after the transfer triggers ≈ $0 capital gains tax. Keeping and renting instead forfeits none of the step-up on a later sale, but subjects the property to decades of property tax, insurance, and maintenance instead.

Planning implication

The federal exemption is currently $15M/person (2026) and inflation-indexed, but future Congresses can change it. If the estate (house + cash inheritance + other assets) may exceed the exemption when the transfer occurs (~2040–2050), model both scenarios: estate tax due vs. exempt. Have the estate plan reviewed by an estate attorney and CPA well before the transfer date.
