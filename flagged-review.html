Privacy standard applied on this site

Personal information is the top priority. No personal names, street addresses, ZIP codes, parcel numbers, contact details, leases, trust/heir details, or personal financial amounts are published. Property-specific values are shown as [WITHHELD — privacy] or blank. Everything below lists what is withheld or unverified and the official route for private verification.

C. Irregularities and incidents

  • Privacy incident (remediated): The first build of this site (PR #1, merged to main on 2026-11-06) published property-specific figures and ZIP/location details on the public GitHub Pages site, conflicting with the user's highest-priority privacy instruction. This commit removes those figures from all public pages; they are replaced with [WITHHELD — privacy] placeholders. Git history still contains the pre-remediation content. Complete removal from history requires repository-owner action (e.g., history-rewrite tooling plus force-push); ordinary commits do not erase it. Flagged for the owner's decision.
  • Consistency: Owner-provided tax/assessed-value figures could not be matched to official parcel records without parcel identifiers, which were not supplied and are intentionally not recorded publicly.
  • Data vintage: Official figures (CDI 2025 data call; NAIC 2023; SJWC 2026 tariffs; SFPUC FY 2026–28 notice) measure different things in different years and are not combined into a trend.
  • Future law: The federal estate tax exclusion ($15M per person in 2026, indexed) is current law but can change by future legislation; track at congress.gov and irs.gov. California Prop 19 rules are current as of the 2026 BOE guidance; track at boe.ca.gov/prop19.

How to close these items

  1. Privately record parcel APN(s), trust documents, bills, leases, and insurance declarations (do not add them to this public site).
  2. Pull enrolled assessed values and prior tax bills from the county Assessor/Treasurer-Tax Collector for each property.
  3. Obtain insurance quotes and utility statements for each property.
  4. Recompute the projection formulas on the projections page with the verified values.
  5. Review the estate against the federal estate tax exclusion with a CPA and estate attorney before the transfer.
