How sources are used

The rule

Every published figure is either (a) taken from an official source listed below, (b) derived arithmetically from official figures, (c) supplied by the owner and labelled as such, or (d) left blank and flagged. Every table cell that carries a value shows a source chip that links to the official document it came from.

What counts as official

Statutes and legislative text from the California Legislature (leginfo); state agency publications from the IRS, the Franchise Tax Board, the Board of Equalization, the Department of Insurance and the State Board of Equalization; county and city government publications from Santa Clara County and the City and County of San Francisco; and published utility rate schedules filed with the California Public Utilities Commission.

What does not

News articles, law-firm commentary, insurance-industry blogs and market data aggregators were used during research to find official sources, but they are not cited as the authority for any published number. Where only a secondary source could be found for a figure, the figure is published as unverified and flagged rather than presented as fact.

All sources every one opened and read during the build

Research notes worth keeping

  • There is no Cupertino Water District. The city’s own bulletin confirms three separate water systems in Cupertino: the City-owned Cupertino Water System (leased to San Jose Water Company), California Water Service in the north-east, and San Jose Water Company in the south-east. The tariff used on the operating-costs page is the San Jose Water Company CPUC General Metered Service schedule, whose territory includes “portions of Cupertino”.
  • The Santa Clara County Tax Rate Book is large — 816 tax rate areas across Sections I and III. The Cupertino parcel’s own TRA total was not located in the extraction, so the base case uses the County Assessor’s published “typical Tax Rate Area” total of $1.1488 per $100, with the observed book range carried as a sensitivity band (flag F5).
  • Two published sources disagree about the San Francisco top transfer-tax tiers — the current city page and the Assessor-Recorder page both show $27.50 and $30.00 per $500 (5.50% and 6.00%), while a 2020 voter digest shows the pre-2020 figures. The current figures are used, and the pending BUILD Act is tracked on the tax thresholds page.
  • Rev. Proc. 2025-32 is the single most efficient federal source. One document supplies the 2026 basic exclusion ($15,000,000), the annual gift exclusion ($19,000), the 2026 ordinary and capital-gain rate tables and the 2026 excess business loss threshold.
  • Leginfo renders an empty Code Text body for repealed sections. R&T §§13311 and 13312 render empty; R&T §13301 — the actual prohibition — renders in full and is the citation used.
  • The Santa Clara County Assessor booklet is the cleanest official statement of Prop 13 mechanics available, and it settles the question of when inheritance is reassessed: as of the date of death, not the date of distribution, and in the name of the estate even if the property is sold rather than distributed.

How to read the badges

  • official confirmed against a government or official rate source
  • owner supplied by the owner — not verified
  • derived computed from other stated figures
  • assumption a planning assumption with no official source
  • unverified could not be confirmed — left blank
  • pending not yet published or not yet in force

Privacy

No name, street address, parcel number, account number or any other personal identifier appears anywhere on this site. The two properties are referred to only as PROPERTY_CUPERTINO and PROPERTY_SF; the owner as OWNER.
