The headline

There is no inheritance tax

California prohibits it by statute. Revenue and Taxation Code §13301, in Part 8 — “Prohibition of Gift and Death Taxes” — states:

“Neither the state nor any political subdivision of the state shall impose any gift, inheritance, succession, legacy, income, or estate tax, or any other tax, on gifts or on the estate or inheritance of any person or on or by reason of any transfer occurring by reason of a death.”

Repealed and added 8 June 1982 by initiative Proposition 6; reenacted in identical language by Stats. 1982, Ch. 1535.

And no federal estate tax here

The federal estate tax applies only above a basic exclusion of $15,000,000 per person for 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.14, implementing P.L. 119-21). The combined value of both properties is far below that, so no Form 706 is required unless portability is wanted for a surviving spouse.

The exclusion is permanent and is adjusted for inflation for 2027 and later years. The 2026 annual gift exclusion is $19,000 per donee (Rev. Proc. 2025-32 §4.42).

So what DOES the inheritance cost?

Two things, and only two things: property tax reassessment and tax on a later sale. The rest of this page explains exactly how each works, how much each would cost for these two properties, and what can be done about it. The reassessment question is worth roughly $44,000–$59,000 a year on PROPERTY_CUPERTINO and $39,000 a year on PROPERTY_SF — far more than any gift or estate tax would ever be.

Thresholds at federal, state and local level

What actually preserves the property tax base

Proposition 19 is a partial exclusion for a parent-to-child transfer of a principal residence. Everything else about the transfer is full reassessment to market value. The rules below come from the statute, the BOE’s Prop 19 materials and the BOE’s trust guidance.

The gap in Prop 19 that matters here

The Prop 19 allowance is the existing taxable value plus an indexed amount ($1,044,586 for transfers between 16 Feb 2025 and 15 Feb 2027, growing at the California CPI every two years thereafter). Everything above that is reassessed. By 2045 the allowance is roughly $1.3 million against a Cupertino market value of roughly $6 million and a taxable value of roughly $800,000 — so the exclusion would leave about $4 million of the value still exposed to reassessment. Prop 19 is a cushion, not a solution. The trust is the solution.

Capital gains, transfer taxes and selling a property

Why a sale after death is cheap but a sale during life may not be

Because both properties sit inside a revocable trust, they are part of the owner’s gross estate, so §1014 gives the heirs a fresh basis at fair market value on the date of death. Selling shortly afterwards therefore taxes only the appreciation since death — roughly one year of growth, about 3.5% of the value. Selling during the owner’s lifetime instead taxes the appreciation since the original purchase, at ordinary income rates, with no step-up. That is the trade-off behind the whole San Francisco decision, and it cannot be quantified until the original purchase basis is supplied (flag F12).
