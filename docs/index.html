The decision, in one screen

  • There is no federal or California estate or inheritance tax on this inheritance. The federal estate tax only applies above $15,000,000 per person in 2026 and California prohibits estate, inheritance and gift taxes by statute (R&T §13301). The real taxes are property tax reassessment and capital-gain tax on a later sale.
  • Everything now depends on one document: the trust. Funding a revocable trust today does not trigger reassessment (R&T §62(d)). Whether the low Prop 13 base survives the owner’s death depends on the vesting language — and the difference is roughly $44,000–$59,000 a year on the Cupertino house alone, depending on whether Prop 19 is available.
  • PROPERTY_CUPERTINO: keep it. If the base is preserved, the property tax in 2045 is about $9,000 a year on an asset worth about $6.0 million. If it is reassessed to market value it becomes $68,000. Living in it keeps the Prop 19 claim alive as a second line of defence.
  • PROPERTY_SF: sell it after the inheritance, unless counsel confirms the base survives. A three-unit building with two tenancies is not the owner’s “family home”, so no Prop 19 exclusion is expected. Reassessed to market value, the property tax goes from about $2,000 to about $41,000 a year and net operating income collapses from about $69,000 to about $30,000.
  • Do not sell PROPERTY_CUPERTINO at the inheritance. Selling costs roughly 2% plus 0.11% state transfer tax plus tax on a small post-death gain — and gives up a very cheap, appreciating shelter. Sell only if the trust base is lost or if liquidity is needed.

Why the trust is the whole answer

Funding the trust is free of reassessment

Revenue and Taxation Code §62(d): a transfer to a trust that is revocable, or in which the trustor is a present beneficiary, is not a change of ownership. Deeding both properties into the trust today keeps their assessed values and issues no supplemental tax bill.

One small cost: recording the deed terminates the Homeowners’ Exemption for the coming fiscal year (worth about $80). File it again.

But death is the trigger

BOE Legal Letter Advice 80/25: when a revocable trust becomes irrevocable, the vesting of an interest in the property in anyone other than the trustor or the trustor’s spouse is a change of ownership. A later distribution out of the trust to a child is a parent-to-child transfer subject to Prop 19 (BOE Legal Opinion 220.0791).

So the trust must continue for the beneficiaries, not vest outright at death. That is a drafting instruction, and it needs a written opinion.

And the county reassesses on the date of death

The Santa Clara County Assessor states that “the reassessment of property acquired by inheritance from an estate or living trust occurs as of the date of the death of the former owner, not on the date of distribution to the beneficiary” — and that an assessment is made in the name of the estate even if the property is sold rather than distributed.

Which means the trust drafting is what determines whether that reassessment means $9,000 a year or $68,000.

What to do next, in order

  1. Pull the official parcel records

    official · Now · 1 month

    One lookup each for PROPERTY_CUPERTINO (Santa Clara County Assessor: parcel number, tax rate area, assessed value) and PROPERTY_SF (San Francisco Treasurer portal: assessed value and the ad valorem tax line). This closes four of the sixteen open flags at once and replaces the illustrative assessed value used in the Cupertino model.

  2. Get a written trust-drafting opinion from California estate counsel

    official · Now · the highest-value action on this page

    Ask for an opinion on the vesting language that keeps the trust alive for the beneficiaries, whether the Cupertino house qualifies as the transferor’s principal residence for Prop 19, what happens if a beneficiary stops occupying it, and whether the County Assessor will pre-confirm the treatment. Do not fund the trust until this is settled.

  3. Decide the San Francisco strategy and get the one number that unlocks it

    official · Now

    Selling during the owner’s lifetime avoids reassessment completely, but taxes the appreciation since purchase at ordinary rates. That comparison needs the original purchase price and date for PROPERTY_SF — which is not yet supplied, so the site cannot quantify it (flag F12).

  4. Get insurance quotes for both properties

    official · Now · 1–2 months

    An HO-3 for the Cupertino house and a DP-3 for the San Francisco building. The premiums are the largest blank in the cost model, and the owner’s $1,500 estimate is at the very bottom of the plausible range.

  5. Fund the trust, re-file the Homeowners’ Exemption, and calendar the annual dates

    official · Now

    Jan 1 lien date, Feb 15 exemption deadline, Dec 10 and Apr 10 instalments, Jul 2 – Sep 15 appeal window, and the biennial BOE adjustment to the Prop 19 allowance. The full checklist is on the process page.
