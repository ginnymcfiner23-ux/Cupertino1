How California property tax works

The 1% cap

“The maximum amount of property tax cannot exceed 1% of a property’s taxable value, plus bonds approved by the voters, service fees, improvement bonds, and special assessments.”

Santa Clara County Office of the Assessor, 2026 owner booklet.

The 2% cap

“Except for changes in ownership or new construction, the base year value increase is limited to 2% per year (or less if the California Consumer Price Index is below 2%).”

Same source. This is why a long-held property keeps a small tax bill.

Change of ownership

“A new base year value is set by reassessment whenever there is a change of ownership or new construction.” And inheritance from an estate or living trust is reassessed as of the date of death.

Same source. This is the hinge of the whole plan.

Reassessment at the inheritance live at the inheritance year you select on the scenarios page

The single most important number on this site

At the inheritance year, each property can end up in one of three states: the taxable value is preserved (the trust kept it), it is partly preserved (Prop 19 applies to the Cupertino house and the allowance buys a small piece), or it is reset to market value. Privacy

No name, street address, parcel number, account number or any other personal identifier appears anywhere on this site. The two properties are referred to only as PROPERTY_CUPERTINO and PROPERTY_SF; the owner as OWNER.

This dashboard is decision-support analysis, not legal, tax or investment advice. Every figure is either traced to the official source linked beside it or flagged as unverified. The figures marked “owner” are the owner’s own estimates and have not been independently confirmed.
