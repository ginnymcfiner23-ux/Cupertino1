The trust decision tree

One question decides the plan

If the trust is drafted so that no interest in the real property vests in the beneficiaries at the owner’s death, does the Santa Clara County assessed value survive?

The answer changes roughly $44,000–$59,000 a year on PROPERTY_CUPERTINO and roughly $39,000 a year on PROPERTY_SF. Everything else on this site is downstream of that answer, and it is a question for a California estate-planning attorney, not for a spreadsheet. Get it answered in writing before funding anything.

If the answer is YES

  • PROPERTY_CUPERTINO — keep it. Live in it or rent it. The property tax stays near $9,000–$10,000 a year in 2045 on a $6 million asset.
  • PROPERTY_SF — hold and rent it. Net operating income of roughly $50,000 a year on a $3.5 million asset is a strong, tax-efficient position.
  • Prop 19 becomes a bonus rather than a necessity, so the heirs are not forced into the 1-year occupancy and 3-year claim deadlines.
  • The §1014 basis step-up still applies because the properties sit inside the decedent’s revocable trust and are therefore in the gross estate.

If the answer is NO

  • PROPERTY_SF — sell it after the inheritance. Reassessed to market value it costs roughly $39,000 a year in extra tax and the building’s yield collapses. Selling after death still gets the §1014 step-up, so the taxable gain is only the appreciation since death.
  • PROPERTY_CUPERTINO — live in it and claim Prop 19, which at least caps part of the reassessment; or sell it if the Prop 19 claim is not worth the 1-year occupancy obligation.
  • Alternatively, sell PROPERTY_SF during the owner’s lifetime, which avoids reassessment entirely — but that cannot be quantified until the original purchase basis is supplied (flag F12).

Privacy

No name, street address, parcel number, account number or any other personal identifier appears anywhere on this site. The two properties are referred to only as PROPERTY_CUPERTINO and PROPERTY_SF; the owner as OWNER.

This dashboard is decision-support analysis, not legal, tax or investment advice. Every figure is either traced to the official source linked beside it or flagged as unverified. The figures marked “owner” are the owner’s own estimates and have not been independently confirmed.
