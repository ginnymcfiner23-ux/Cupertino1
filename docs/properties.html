The two rules that govern everything

1. The base year value only moves at 2% a year

“Except for changes in ownership or new construction, the base year value increase is limited to 2% per year (or less if the California Consumer Price Index is below 2%).” That is why an owner-held property keeps a small, predictable tax bill no matter how much the market moves.

Santa Clara County Office of the Assessor, Assessment Information for the Santa Clara County Property Owner (2026).

2. A change of ownership resets the base year to market value

“A new base year value is set by reassessment whenever there is a change in ownership or new construction.” And: “The reassessment of property acquired by inheritance from an estate or living trust occurs as of the date of the death of the former owner, not on the date of distribution to the beneficiary.”

Same source. This is the rule the whole plan is designed around — see the tax thresholds page for the exceptions.
