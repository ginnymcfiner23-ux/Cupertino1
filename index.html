Plan a California property inheritance with less uncertainty.

A public, privacy-preserving research dashboard about deciding whether to occupy, rent, or sell inherited residential property, with California property-tax rules and federal transfer-tax anchors. Jurisdiction-specific references are included for relevant county and city laws, but no individual property is linked to a location; all property-specific figures and identifiers are withheld.

Bottom line: No defensible “highest-profit” choice can yet be calculated. Trust terms, transferor occupancy, parcel assessments, insurance quotes, maintenance history, net rental cash flow, and future law are unknown or unverified. Preserve the option to occupy the inherited family home until a qualified estate/property-tax review confirms whether a Proposition 19 residence claim is available. Do not sell or change tenancy arrangements until an after-tax comparison and tenant-law review are complete.

At a glance

What matters most

Property tax is a major unknown

California generally resets assessed value after a change in ownership. A qualifying parent-to-child family-home exclusion requires the transferor’s principal residence to become the transferee’s principal residence within one year, subject to a value limit. Renting the home instead may mean reassessment; the county Assessor must decide from the trust, facts, and transfer date.

See Prop 19 rules →

Rental income is not net profit

Compare collected rent after vacancy, taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs, capital work, and income tax. Tenant protections can constrain occupancy and re-rental decisions. Property-specific cash flow and unit details are intentionally withheld.

See operating-cost framework →

Estate tax is a portfolio-wide test

For 2026, the federal basic exclusion is $15 million per decedent, with lifetime taxable gifts and possible deceased-spouse unused exclusion affecting the applicable amount. This is not an inheritance tax charged to the beneficiary, and the 2040–50 threshold is unknown.

Verified anchors

Known rules vs. blank forecasts

Known as of the review date
  • California Prop 13 generally limits annual assessed-value increases to the lower of inflation or 2%, except ownership change/new construction and Proposition 8 decline-in-value conditions. SF official explainer
  • 2026 federal estate basic exclusion: $15,000,000; 2027 published amount: $15,480,000. IRS estate-tax FAQ · IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-35
  • Inherited asset basis is generally date-of-death FMV, subject to exceptions. IRS Pub. 551
Intentionally withheld or left blank

Property values, tax histories, rent, utility costs, insurance, repairs and 2040–2050 forecasts are private or unverified. Future property prices, local tax rates, insurance premiums and tax thresholds cannot be verified. The public tables use placeholders and identify the official route for private verification.

Immediate actions

Reduce uncertainty before the transfer

1. Estate and trust file

Have an estate attorney review title, trust amendments, successor trustee authority, beneficiaries, debts, gifts and prior Form 706 / portability records.

2. Property baselines

Privately obtain assessor values/tax bills, insurance declarations, utility statements, rental agreements, repair invoices and legal unit/permit records.

3. Compare after tax

Model hold/live, hold/rent, and sell/reinvest using verified net cash flows and actual tax basis; review each year and again at the actual transfer date.
