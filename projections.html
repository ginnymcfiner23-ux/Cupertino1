Why selling generally comes out ahead on this property

  • Step-up in basis: selling near the date of death means ≈ $0 capital gains tax (basis resets to market value). [IRS Pub 551]
  • Prop 19 makes keeping expensive: renting the Cupertino house triggers full reassessment → ≈ $58,000–81,000/year property tax alone by 2045–2050 (mid case), plus insurance, maintenance, and management. Even moving in as primary residence leaves ≈ $57,000–70,000/year tax because the market value dwarfs the assessed value + $1M cap.
  • Rent would not cover costs: at the 2045 mid case, tax + insurance + maintenance alone ≈ $132,000/year (≈ $11,000/month) before management and vacancies — above plausible market rent for a single-family home (market rent not verified; owner to obtain a rental appraisal if considering this option).
  • SF home is the better keep: it carries a low assessed value (≈ $2,439/year tax per owner records) and ≈ $7,150/month rental income while the owner lives in the third unit — strong positive cash flow.

Recommendation (illustrative — not financial, legal, or tax advice)

Plan A (preferred): Sell the Cupertino house shortly after the trust transfer (~2040–2050). Use the step-up in basis to sell with little or no capital gains tax, and reinvest the net proceeds into a diversified portfolio (stocks, savings, bonds) per your risk tolerance.

Plan B: Only keep the Cupertino house if an heir needs it as a primary residence — and then expect property tax of ≈ $24,000+/year at 2026 values rising to ≈ $57,000–70,000+/year by 2045–2050 (mid case), plus insurance and maintenance. Do not keep it as a rental: under Prop 19 it would be fully reassessed and the carrying cost likely exceeds achievable rent.

Keep the San Francisco home: primary residence + two rented units (≈ $7,150/month) is a strong cash-flow position; do not give it up to move to Cupertino unless family circumstances require it.

Before the transfer: confirm the trust holds title to the house; obtain a date-of-death appraisal; file BOE-266/BOE-19-P on time if keeping; review the estate against the federal exemption ($15M/person in 2026, indexed) with a CPA and estate attorney; re-run these projections with actual figures.
