Recommendation

Under current law and these projections, sell the Cupertino home shortly after inheritance and reinvest the proceeds, while keeping the San Francisco home as the primary residence with its rental income.

RECOMMENDED: Sell & reinvest — highest projected net income, lowest cost and complexity.
  • Why sell: Proposition 19 removes the old "keep the parents' low tax base" benefit for anyone who doesn't move in. Holding the Cupertino home means property taxes of roughly $6,200–7,700/month by 2045 (plus insurance and maintenance), while selling near date-of-death value triggers little or no capital gains tax (stepped-up basis) and puts ~$7.4M to work — roughly $24,800/month at a 4% withdrawal rate.
  • Why not rent: Rentals receive no Prop 19 exclusion → full reassessment → ~$92,436/year taxes. Break-even rent is estimated at ~$15,200/month before income taxes, insurance, and maintenance; achievable rent was not verified and may not cover costs.
  • Why not live in it (unless you want to): Only sensible if an heir actually wants to live in Cupertino and can carry ~$6,200/month in property taxes plus insurance and maintenance. It also ends the SF home's primary-residence status (though a 3rd rentable unit becomes available).
Key uncertainties: future appreciation, tax law (Prop 19 repeal initiative for Nov 2026 ballot is pending; federal estate-tax rules could change), insurance cost for a multi-million-dollar home, achievable rent, and actual maintenance.
