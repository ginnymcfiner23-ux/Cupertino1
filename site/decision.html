Decision & plan

Live, rent, or sell-and-reinvest after taxes, with break-even tests and a sensitivity check.

Not tax, legal, or investment advice. Projections are illustrative. Values marked USER-PROVIDED are inputs not verified against an official source. Blank cells are unverified and flagged.

Method

Each option is compared over 10 years after inheritance, in nominal after-tax dollars. Sell: sell at market value less 5% cost, no tax on gain (basis steps up to value at death), invest at an after-tax return. Live (child qualifies under Prop 19): pay the reassessed-but-capped tax, insurance, water, net electricity, and maintenance, and forgo investment income on the equity; sell at year 10 with capital-gains tax. Rent / no move-in: full reassessment. B keep: rental NOI after tax (35% assumed marginal rate, 27.5-year depreciation on 80% building share) and sale at year 10 with recapture. Break-even rent shows what avoided or earned rent would be needed to match selling; it is not a claim about the market.

Recommendation and reasons

  1. Sell both, soon after inheritance, unless the heir will live in Property A. The sale step-up means little or no gain at sale; holding adds annual carrying costs and a reassessment tax step, while the sale value is set at inheritance.
  2. Property B is the clearest sell. Its 2.135% effective rate (user bill) on a reassessed market value makes the first-year tax about $61,431 (2045 base), and NOI before tax is thin. Keeping it is worth less after 10 years in every scenario.
  3. Property A is a sell unless it is lived in. Living there requires a break-even avoided rent of roughly $19,346/mo in the 2045 base case. No market rent was supplied; the comparison is a threshold, not a verdict.
  4. If the heir does live in Property A, the Prop 19 cap limits the tax step to about $52,225 in year 1 (2045 base), versus about $66,173 without it. The child must move in within one year.

Caveats: market values, water/electric/insurance costs, the solar credit, and every rate on the projection and decision inputs are unverified (see Flagged for Review). Nothing here is tax or legal advice.
