Bottom line

Under current law (Proposition 19, in force since Feb 16, 2021), an heir who inherits a California home must move into it as a primary residence within one year and file the proper claims to keep any part of the parent's low Prop 13 tax base. Even then, only roughly the first $1 million of value above the old assessed value is protected. At a projected ~$7.8M value in the mid-2040s, keeping the Cupertino home means property taxes of roughly $6,200/month (if occupied) to $7,700/month (if rented), plus insurance and maintenance. Renting would require about $15,200/month in rent just to break even before income taxes — an unverified rent assumption.

Selling shortly after inheritance produces little or no capital gains tax because inherited property receives a stepped-up basis to its date-of-death value (IRC §1014). Net proceeds (est. ~$7.4M after 5% selling costs at projected values) reinvested could generate roughly $24,800/month at a 4% withdrawal rate — far above the cost of holding — while the household keeps its San Francisco home and its $85,800/year rental income.

Projected recommendation: Sell the Cupertino home shortly after inheritance and reinvest the proceeds. Keep the San Francisco home as primary residence + rental income. Re-visit this analysis at the time of inheritance; tax law (Prop 19, the federal estate-tax exemption) may change.
