Executive summary

  • Federal estate tax is not the main issue for these two properties. The 2026 basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per person (IRS). The two properties total about $4,900,000 in user-estimated value, which leaves roughly $10,100,000 of headroom for other estate assets before debts, expenses, and prior gifts (see Inheritance steps).
  • California adds no inheritance or estate tax on these assets (research basis: secondary sources; official confirmation flagged, F-11). Rental income and sale gains remain taxable.
  • Prop 19 is the largest cost driver. Inherited property keeps its old assessment only if a child moves in within one year. Otherwise it is reassessed to market. In the base case (inheritance 2045), Property A's first-year tax is about $66,173 if reassessed versus $52,225 if the child qualifies, against $7,207 today.
  • Property B's rental reassessment is steep. In the base case, its first-year tax is about $61,431 after reassessment, against $2,439 today (the rent-controlled units' treatment is flagged, F-17).
  • Sell-and-invest beat both living in A and renting out B in the low, base, and high scenarios (see Decision & plan). The gap is driven by the after-tax investment return assumption, which is labeled, not verified.

Best plan (based on the model)

  1. Sell Property B soon after inheritance (base 2045: sell-and-invest ≈ $4,495,487 vs keep-as-rental ≈ $3,395,353 after 10 years). Reassessment alone makes the rent-controlled building costly to hold.
  2. Sell Property A soon after inheritance unless a child will actually live there within a year and the housing value is worth more than the break-even avoided rent (base 2045: about $19,346/mo). No market rent was supplied, so that figure is a threshold, not a finding.
  3. Keep the step-up: value both properties at date-of-death market value and keep the appraisal (IRS basis rules; F-06).
  4. Verify before acting: the flagged items on the Flagged for Review page, especially market values, water provider, and the Prop 19 treatment of a multi-unit property.

Scenario labels: base = 2.5% inflation, 2.5% home growth, 6% nominal investment return; low and high are on the decision page. All are labeled assumptions.
