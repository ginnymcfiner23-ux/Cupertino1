Bottom line

Under current law (Proposition 19, in force since Feb 16, 2021), an heir who inherits a California home must move into it as a primary residence within one year and file the proper claims to keep any part of the parent's low Prop 13 tax base. Even then, only roughly the first $1 million of value above the old assessed value is protected (inflation-adjusted cap). Because the Cupertino home's market value far exceeds its low assessed value, keeping it means materially higher property taxes after inheritance — roughly the current tax bill scaled up by the reassessment (exact figures depend on the date-of-death value and are masked here).

Renting it out triggers full reassessment to market value (rentals receive no Prop 19 exclusion), so the break-even rent must cover the reassessed property tax plus insurance, water, electricity, and maintenance — before income taxes. Selling shortly after inheritance produces little or no capital gains tax because inherited property receives a stepped-up basis to its date-of-death value (IRC §1014). Net proceeds reinvested can produce ongoing investment income, while the household keeps its San Francisco home and its rental income.

Projected recommendation: Sell the Cupertino home shortly after inheritance and reinvest the proceeds. Keep the San Francisco home as primary residence + rental income. Re-visit this analysis at the time of inheritance; tax law (Prop 19, the federal estate-tax exemption) may change.
