Taxes on Inheriting & Holding the Cupertino Home

Three tax layers apply: federal estate tax, California state taxes, and local property tax. California's property-tax rules (Prop 13 / Prop 19) are the biggest lever in this decision by far.

What this means: An estate below $15M per person ($30M per married couple with portability) owes no federal estate tax. The Cupertino home alone (est. $3.1M today; projected ~$7.8M by ~2045) does not approach the threshold, but the inheritance "includes a considerable amount of money" — if the total estate exceeds the exemption in effect at death, 40% applies only to the excess and is paid by the estate before distribution, not by heirs. The exemption was made permanent starting in 2026 by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21) and is indexed for inflation after 2027 — but future Congresses could change it. 3. Property tax — Proposition 13 & Proposition 19

California property tax = assessed value × combined tax rate (1% Prop 13 base + voter-approved debt + local levies). Assessed value rises at most 2%/year while ownership is unchanged — but changes hands on death, which triggers reassessment unless an exclusion applies.

Why this dominates the decision: The Cupertino home carries a very low assessed value (~$550K vs ~$3.1M market). If inherited by a child who does not move in, it is reassessed to ~market value and property taxes roughly quintuple. Even moving in only shields roughly the first $1M+ of the gap.
